\begin{theorem} A smallest counterexample to the Berge\textendash{}Fulkerson conjecture cannot have a partition into four vertex sets, each of which induces a subgraph containing a cycle and has exactly five edges leaving it.
\end{theorem}

A Berge\textendash{}Fulkerson coloring gives each edge a two-element subset of six colors. At every cubic vertex the three subsets partition the colors, so each color supplies a perfect matching. The boundary profile of a pole is the set of realizable assignments to its semiedges up to renaming the six colors. Represent an assignment by its six color supports on the ports, sorted as integers.

\section{Reduction to one connection pattern}

Suppose a smallest counterexample has the four parts $P_0,P_1,P_2,P_3$. It is cyclically 5-edge-connected by the small-cut reduction. Each part boundary is a cycle-separating five-edge cut. Its terminals on each side of the cut are distinct. If a vertex met at least two cut edges, moving it across the cut would shrink the cut and preserve a cycle on each side. The vertex originally has degree at most one on its own side, so it belongs to no cycle there. \href{https://iris.univr.it/handle/11562/1030915}{Small-cut reduction}

Write $m_{ij}$ for the number of edges between parts $i$ and $j$. The four degree equations $\sum_{j\ne i}m_{ij}=5$ give $m_{01}=m_{23}$, $m_{02}=m_{13}$, and $m_{03}=m_{12}$. The boundary of $P_i\cup P_j$ is cycle-separating and has size $10-2m_{ij}$, so $m_{ij}\leq2$. The three multiplicities at a part sum to five. They are therefore 2, 2, and 1. Relabel the parts so the pairs of multiplicity two are $01,02,13,23$ and the pairs of multiplicity one are $03,12$.

In Proposition 4.4 of M\'{a}\v{c}ajov\'{a}, Mazzuoccolo and Tabarelli, cases I\textendash{}XII require a side that is a literal pentagon. Its complement includes the other three cyclic parts, so only a part itself can be a pentagon. Thus a part has the 16-type pentagon profile or one of the corrected complementary profiles of case XIII, with 26 or 30 types. There are 12 distinct port orders of the pentagon profile and six of each complementary profile, giving 24 choices for a labeled part. \href{https://doi.org/10.26493/1855-3974.3409.c13}{Proposition 4.4} \enspace \href{https://github.com/claude-make-no-mistakes/berge-fulkerson-counterexample-search/blob/main/FIVE-CUT-TARGETS.md}{Corrected classification}

\section{Composing two profiles}

Label the ports of $P_0$ and $P_1$ so ports 0 and 1 connect in order, and do the same for $P_2,P_3$. The six external ports of each half are the three remaining ports of its first part followed by those of its second part. Number them 0 through 5. In the left half the ports $0,1$ connect to $P_2$ and port 2 to $P_3$. Ports $3,4$ connect to $P_3$ and port 5 to $P_2$. In the right half choose the orders so right ports $0,\ldots,5$ connect to left ports $0,1,5,3,4,2$, respectively. Every possible local port labeling is already among the 24 profiles.

For a pair of five-port assignments, the two port labels on the join are ordered pairs of two-element sets. Up to color names, they are determined by whether the sets intersect in zero, one, or two colors. In each class choose a fixed ordered pair of sets. For each constituent profile, enumerate its types and all color permutations that send its joining labels to the fixed pair. Record every resulting assignment on the remaining three ports.

For each class take all pairs of the recorded three-port assignments. Their concatenations, with color names forgotten, give exactly the six-port profile of the combined pole. Every recorded pair comes from realizable assignments with equal labels on both joined edges. Conversely a coloring of the combined pole restricts to constituent colorings whose join labels agree and can be normalized by a common color permutation. The calculation only uses the boundary profiles.

Applying this composition to the 24 profile choices gives 576 entries per half, with 288 distinct sets. Apply the fixed port permutation to the right half and compare all $24^4=331{,}776$ choices. Every intersection contains at least 27 types. A common type glues the two colorings into a cover of the smallest counterexample, a contradiction. \href{https://github.com/claude-make-no-mistakes/berge-fulkerson-counterexample-search/blob/main/four-five-poles.jsonl}{Exact replay}
